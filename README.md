# The Perils of Machiavellianism — Personal Notes Summary

## Overview
This repository contains a concise summary of my working thesis on Machiavellianism as a civilizational contagion. It serves as my own reference point and as an orientation layer for anyone who wants to explore deeper material across my related repositories.

## Summary
**Healthy civilizations depend on trust.**  
Trust is the basic operating condition that allows cooperation, institutions, and social stability.

**Machiavellianism destroys that foundation.**  
A Machiavellian will say or do anything to get what they want, and this corrodes the trust‑matrix that civilization depends on.

**If Machiavellians gain control, the worst actors rise.**  
Power selects for the cruelest and most manipulative individuals because they cross lines others won’t.

**Containment only works early.**  
Civilized societies can uproot Machiavellianism when institutions are strong, but once Machiavellians dominate, no peaceful mechanism can remove them.

**“I’m gonna get mine” is the gateway.**  
This mentality is the first step toward Machiavellian thinking and erodes cooperative norms.

**Machiavellianism spreads like a contagion.**  
It exploits trust asymmetry: trusting people expose themselves, Machiavellians exploit that trust, and the behavior spreads.

**Adaptive pressure forces people to imitate it.**  
Once Machiavellianism becomes common, non‑predatory individuals must adopt similar tactics or withdraw.

**Weak institutions accelerate the spread.**  
When transparency and accountability decline, Machiavellian strategies become profitable and easier to embed.

**Narrative control locks in their power.**  
Once in charge, Machiavellians maintain dominance by controlling information and shaping perception.

**Micro‑trust networks become the only refuge.**  
In a contaminated environment, small clusters of trustworthy people are the only way to preserve cooperation.
